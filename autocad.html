3-Phase Motor Control Schematic

An electrical control schematic for a conveyor motor, drawn in AutoCAD Electrical: a 3-phase power circuit, a 120V control circuit, and a PLC input tying them together. Completed as the mini project of an AutoCAD Electrical course.

About this project

This is a guided course project, not an original design. I followed the course's mini project to draw a complete control schematic for a conveyor motor, and the drawing above is the result. I include it because it shows I can build a correctly structured electrical schematic in the industry-standard tool, which is a different skill from the PCB-level design in my other projects.

The power circuit

The left half of the drawing is the 3-phase power path. Three power lines on rungs 100 to 102 pass through a circuit breaker (CB100), individual fuses (FU108 to FU110), and the contacts of a motor starter, then through an overload and a disconnect (DISC108) to the conveyor motor (MTR108), which is also bonded to ground. The three phases are drawn as separate wires, with the red, yellow and blue colours coming from the wire layers in the project.

The control circuit

The right half runs at 120V, stepped down from the 480V supply by a control transformer (T116). A normally closed stop button (PB120A) and a start button (PB120) feed the motor starter coil (M120), and a running light (LT122) is wired in parallel so it lights whenever the starter is energised. A 15A breaker (CB125) protects the control side.

The PLC input

The 120V control signal also goes to a PLC input module, an Allen-Bradley 1771 isolated discrete input card (PLC126). The connection between the two circuits is made with signal arrows ("from 112" and "to 126") rather than a drawn wire, which keeps the sheet readable. The starter contact on the power side is cross-referenced to its coil on the control side, so each part points to the other.

What I learned in AutoCAD Electrical

  • Setting up a project and drawing with the ladder reference system, so every component sits on a numbered rung.
  • Inserting components from the icon menu, with tags assigned automatically from the rung number.
  • Wire layers by gauge and colour, and wire numbers that follow the connections.
  • Linking coils and contacts with cross-references, and connecting circuits with signal arrows.
  • Building a parametric PLC module and wiring its inputs.
  • Linking the schematic to a panel layout through component footprints, and plotting the whole project to PDF.
